Do not present every module to every user. Navigation is determined by role and current task.

## 6. Member dashboard

Answer:
- Is membership active?
- What needs attention?
- What opportunities are available?
- What is new?
- What learning/mentorship is pending?
- What events/projects can I join?

Suggested sections:
membership status, profile completeness, verification status, opportunities, upcoming events, mentorship and learning progress, project participation.

## 7. Profile UX

Show headline, summary, verification indicators, experience, education, skills, services, certifications, portfolio, availability and MPA participation.

Do not make registration a huge form. Use progressive completion:
account -> minimum profile -> membership -> verification -> professional depth.

## 8. Organization UX

Organizations should quickly post jobs, find professionals, request services, publish events and propose projects.

Do not force organization users through member-only language.

## 9. Search

Professional search should support natural goals such as finding a civil engineer in Addis Ababa, a project manager with construction experience or a finance trainer.

Search must be backed by structured fields.

## 10. Verification UX

States:
Not Submitted
Submitted
Under Review
Correction Required
Verified
Expired
Rejected

Correction must identify the exact field/document, reviewer comment, required action and deadline where applicable.

## 11. Payment UX

Always show what is being paid, amount, purpose, provider, current status, reference and receipt.

Never call a payment successful solely because the browser returned from a payment page.

## 12. Project UX

Public project pages show purpose, location, budget/funding status, progress, beneficiaries, partners, updates and publishable evidence.

Internal project pages additionally show assignments, financial detail, evidence, inspections, corrections and audit.

## 13. Event UX

Show title, organizer, date/time/timezone, location, description, eligibility, capacity, registration and calendar action.

After rescheduling, clearly show the old/new date or venue and provide calendar update.

## 14. Responsive design

Critical flows must work on small screens: registration, membership, payment, job application, event registration, mentorship request and field workflows where device capability permits.

## 15. Accessibility

Use semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, visible focus, readable typography, sufficient contrast and meaningful errors.

## 16. Design language

Member-facing: clean, trustworthy, modern, calm and information-rich without being dense.

Operations: dense enterprise UI is acceptable.

## 17. Empty states

Every module needs a useful next action. Example: “Set a professional goal and request mentorship from an eligible mentor.”

## 18. Error states

Tell users what happened, what was not completed and what they can do next. Never expose raw technical errors.
